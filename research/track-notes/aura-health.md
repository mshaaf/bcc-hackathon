# Track 2 reconsidered for “hella LARP” and “hella aura”

October 20, 2026. Exactly two strong generalist engineers; build October 21–23. This is a positive reading of the new lens: the user should inhabit a consequential role, make choices that matter, and leave with something useful. Aura scores are subjective presentation/identity potential, not rubric weights or evidence of medical benefit. The actual criteria remain track fit, creativity, working functionality, and presentation.

## New verdict

**My Words, My Visit moves narrowly ahead of Reclaim under this lens—but only after becoming an interactive, patient-controlled rehearsal and advocate handoff.** A questionnaire with a voice chatbot added does not earn that change. Reclaim remains the more distinctive evidence story and the safer fallback if the team cannot make practice feel valuable without acting as a clinician. Neither gets to promise better care, restored waiting time, or a successful appeal.

Health is a constrained arena for spectacle. Make the *person's agency* cinematic, not their suffering: no race-based antagonist, crisis countdown, vital-sign alarm, simulated adverse birth, hospital boss fight, or applause for disclosing a real diagnosis. Public participants always use an invented scenario and can skip speech.

## What the flagship actually feels like

**Premise:** “You choose what needs to be heard.” A patient or chosen support person practices the *communication move* they want available to them—not how to behave acceptably to a doctor.

The initial screen asks which goal matters today: finish an important point, ask for an explanation in plain language, or clarify the next step. The user chooses self or supporter; a supporter explicitly agrees to help the patient speak and follow their wishes. They can select a gentle pace, turn off time pressure, skip, or stop. Two reviewed fictional maternal-visit scenarios contain no emergency symptoms or clinical recommendations.

The conversation counterpart uses fixed neutral prompts such as “What would you like to make sure is covered today?” and “What would you like clarified before the conversation ends?” It does not wear a white coat, say a symptom is normal, suggest treatment, or grant medical approval. A busy/limited-time prompt is optional and clearly labeled practice, never sprung on the user.

The visible payoff is a **before/after agenda**, not a score: the person's selected goal, their original concern, the phrasing they chose after practice, one question still unanswered, and their support person's agreed role. The user confirms all of it. AHRQ/CDC source cards support available question prompts; they are not citations to justify a diagnosis. The card is usable even if the rehearsal is skipped or AI is unavailable.

### An honest 90-second demonstration

0–15 seconds: show a fictional routine visit scenario and ask a volunteer judge to choose one of the three goals; never ask for their own health history. 15–35: the engineer responds to the first practice prompt; the app displays the actual input. 35–55: the judge chooses “pause and retry” or “ask for clarification”; the branch actually changes. 55–75: switch to the support-person role, pick “help me finish my question,” and show how it preserves the patient's agenda. 75–90: export the original concern, revised chosen wording, and unanswered question on the real one-page card. End on the artifact, not a fictional doctor saying “you saved your life.”

Text input is a complete working mode. If voice is built, it must truly transcribe and show a correction step. A prerecorded reply or fixed branch is fine when explicitly disclosed; secretly replacing broken realtime behavior with a video is not.

## Why it beats—or loses to—Reclaim

**The Room wins the new preference:** a person inhabits a role, makes a consequential choice, retries, and visibly changes what they will bring into a real conversation. The audience can participate without needing to understand transplantation. It has a repeatable activity rather than a one-time document reveal.

**Reclaim wins specificity:** a few source-backed numbers expose an actual historical policy mechanism and create an unusually memorable evidence reveal. Its role is an investigator/steward, with the team—not the software—making the decision. An honest version must stop before the dramatic outcome people most want, namely “time restored.” That is still compelling, but less participatory unless the evidence inspection is genuinely interactive.

I would choose **The Room** for the user's explicit aura/LARP preference if a community reviewer can assess its language by Thursday. If no such review is possible, or its rehearsal becomes generic persuasion coaching, **choose Reclaim**. Do not compensate by adding a fake clinician or scoring how “confident” a Black patient sounds.

## New competitor check: rehearsal is not novel

- [Yoodli's May 19, 2026 roleplay builder documentation](https://support.yoodli.ai/en/articles/11565137-how-to-build-and-customize-roleplays) already supports custom simulated conversations and feedback. [Its healthcare offering](https://yoodli.ai/industries/healthcare) targets clinicians/teams, but “AI roleplay for healthcare” is already a category.
- [Tender Health's Practice Room](https://www.tenderhealth.ca/practice-room), inspected as a live current page, directly advertises patient voice rehearsal, a health map, Warm/Direct AI doctor modes, and recap. Its undated page does not establish a launch date or clinical efficacy; it is nevertheless important observed product overlap. **An AI doctor to practice your appointment is not our novelty.**
- [HEARD](https://www.myheardapp.com/how-it-works) advertises patient documentation and AI advocate support/preparation. Do not call patient visit prep itself new.
- The only defensible difference proposed here is **patient-selected goals + chosen-supporter role + controllable retry + original-language handoff**, designed with Black maternal experience in view. Even that is a product design hypothesis, not proven defensibility. No factual claim about an “Ellipsis rehearsal product” was verified, so omit that name unless separately established.

## Three-day cut line and evaluation

Engineer A builds the deterministic two-scenario rehearsal, pause/retry, and saved user choices. Engineer B builds accessible mobile presentation, original-language card/export, source cards, and deployment. Both test the sample cases and user wording. Wednesday: functioning choice-to-card flow. Thursday: polish actual role switching, review wording, optionally add voice. Friday: accessibility/uncertainty fixes, user task tests, backup recording, submit.

Cut unrestricted chat, medical persona, 3D/avatar, automated emotional analysis, accent/pacing/confidence ratings, account systems, real clinical integrations, notifications, and multiplayer networking. A two-person live demonstration can inhabit patient and supporter without requiring a shared backend.

Measure whether 3–5 testers can choose a goal, retry a communication move, and produce a card containing their original concern and unanswered question. Confirm no selected goal or source text is silently changed. Report usability and goal completion only. This is not evidence of improved trust, reduced maternal deaths, better therapy access, or any medical outcome.
