# Track 3 finalists: finished-product and presentation concepts

These are visual concepts, not built products. Every mockup should display **Concept preview · fictional demo scenario**. Presentation timings below are a proposed three-minute format, not a verified event rule.

## BLOCK OPS

**Promise and user.** A neighborhood group turns public service-request history, its own observations, and a chosen next step into one usable meeting brief.

**Main screen.** A civic worktable: warm ivory, deep plum typography, sunflower task tabs. The top reads **BLOCK OPS / Riverbend Block Circle**, a fictional group. A narrow left rail holds “Review records → Confirm context → Plan next step.” The center contains a large chronological case table. The right panel shows **Our next move**, role assignments, and available volunteer time. A persistent distinction separates **Official record status** from **Resident observation**. The export button reads **Make our brief**.

**Three meaningful states.**

1. **Review:** four candidate records appear; none is automatically declared the same problem. Clicking a card reveals its source fields and date.
2. **Confirm:** the group removes an unrelated record, selects its own history, and adds a dated observation or leaves present conditions unknown. Counts and timeline update.
3. **Act:** the group chooses a request, assigns two role-based tasks, and exports the brief. This marks the plan prepared, never the issue resolved.

**Fictional mockup copy/data.** Case: **Demo Site A — no real address**. Cards: **DEMO-101 · July 8 · Closed**; **DEMO-102 · July 29 · Closed**; **DEMO-103 · September 3 · Closed**. Summary: **3 selected reports · current condition unverified**. Observation: **“We have not checked the site this week.”** Ask: **“Please explain the recorded closure and the route for requesting follow-up.”** Tasks: **Records keeper — verify IDs, 20 minutes**; **Meeting speaker — review our request, 15 minutes**. Nearby **DEMO-104** is explicitly a different fictional site.

**Proposed presentation.** **Opening, 30 seconds:** “We have several ticket numbers and a meeting tomorrow. Help our group decide what belongs in the record.” **Live interaction, 90 seconds:** a judge acts as records keeper, rejects DEMO-104, inspects a source, and chooses the next action; the presenter adds the fictional observation and assigns tasks. Open the exported brief. **Technical proof, 40 seconds:** inspect a real cached public record separately, show exact date/status preservation, and demonstrate that removing a record changes the output. **Close, 20 seconds:** “Our group chooses the priority and the ask. Block Ops makes the evidence portable.”

**Useful output.** An editable one-page timeline, observation, requested action, task plan, source appendix and snapshot date.

**Prior art/risk.** [DC's To-Do List](https://viridicity.github.io/dc311/) already offers a service dashboard; 311 and SeeClickFix already support reporting. Differentiation is collective review and an actionable brief. Nearby reports do not establish recurrence, and closure does not establish remediation.

**Hard cut: two engineers, three days.** One category, one ward, cached public data, confirmed grouping, one observation, two tasks, print/PDF. No multiplayer, automatic complaints, monitoring, predictions or surveillance. The parent research found 475 matching public records in its specified date window; mockup fixtures must never be presented as those actual records.

## PEOPLE'S CHAMBER

**Promise and user.** A resident rehearses their own public testimony with source-backed hearing context and leaves with an editable statement they can submit themselves.

**Main screen.** A warm ivory practice chamber with oxblood headings and restrained brass accents. Header: **PEOPLE'S CHAMBER / Your voice, prepared**. Persistent label: **Practice session — not an official Council proceeding**. The left panel shows the selected notice and its source. A large center **floor card** holds “My experience / Why it matters / My request.” On the right: a prominent timer, **Give me the floor**, and **Practice question**. No government seal or official-looking verdict.

**Three meaningful states.**

1. **Prepare:** inspect the source context, enter personal experience, and choose an ask. Missing logistics remain visibly unknown.
2. **Take the floor:** the timer runs while the resident speaks; a human chair selects one fixed practice question.
3. **Revise and carry out:** the resident edits their own request, reviews source-backed context, and downloads testimony plus rehearsal notes. The official submission link is a handoff.

**Fictional mockup copy.** Context title: **Neighborhood services — fictional practice notice**. Logistics: **Practice only; no official hearing or deadline**. Experience: **“Our neighborhood group wants clearer explanations when service requests close.”** Request: **“Please describe what action was taken and how residents can request follow-up.”** Fixed chair question: **“What specific action are you asking for?”** Button after revision: **Download my statement**. Confirmation: **Draft downloaded · nothing submitted**.

**Proposed presentation.** **Opening, 30 seconds:** “You have something specific to say. Let's practice making it heard.” **Live, 90 seconds:** a judge plays chair, gives the resident the floor, and asks the fixed question; the resident revises their ask and downloads the statement. **Technical proof, 40 seconds:** reveal the notice citation, distinguish user experience from institutional facts, and show that the revised request appears in the exported document. **Close, 20 seconds:** “The resident owns the words. The practice room helps them arrive prepared.”

**Output, prior art and risk.** Actual testimony export is the planned function; no official submission is simulated as completed. [HMS already supports discovery and submission](https://dccouncil.gov/how-to-testify-before-the-council/). The new value is rehearsal and revision. Avoid generic AI speechwriting or a fake official chair.

**Hard cut.** One verified notice, editable draft, timer, fixed human-chair question, revision and export. Label archived notices. Cut voice agents, recording, transcription, avatars, persuasion scoring and LIMS API dependency. Fictional rehearsal roles are disclosed; document handling and export must genuinely work in the eventual build.
