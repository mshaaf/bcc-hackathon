# BCC project selection — research synthesis

## Summary

For two engineers with three build days and a preference for meaningful judge participation, **Block Ops was the strongest overall recommendation and is now the user's selected project** (October 21, 2026). **The Switch** and **Casebreaker** remain research alternatives. The six-finalist previews make the intended experience concrete, but do not validate demand, institutional outcomes, or winning odds.

## Evidence grading

- **High:** directly observed and reproducible, or supported by multiple independent primary sources. Applies to a precise claim, not an entire product idea.
- **Medium:** a credible primary source or indirect support establishes the mechanism or existing capability, but not the proposed product's effectiveness.
- **Low:** a design hypothesis, unvalidated demand, speculative competitive advantage, or expected judging reaction.

## Six-finalist assessment

- **Block Ops / Community:** best combined fit for local relevance, inspectable engineering, group agency, and a reliable brief. The hard part is responsible grouping and keeping the final request source-faithful. A service map alone is weak differentiation.
- **The Switch / AI:** strongest experiment-driven stage moment. The hard part is reviewed meaning-equivalent inputs, repeat controls, and actual reproducible findings. A passing experiment remains a pass.
- **Casebreaker / AI:** clearest document contradiction and practical evidence packet. The hard part is conservative matching and accurate source spans. The pitch must explain the connection to automated screening without asserting a hidden vendor process.
- **The Room / Healthcare:** strongest patient/supporter roleplay. The hard part is creating usefulness beyond existing rehearsal tools while preserving patient control. No clinical answers or performance grading.
- **Reclaim / Healthcare:** most specific policy-based need and handoff recipient. The hard parts are explanation time, remaining demand, and coordinator usefulness. No time-credit prediction.
- **People's Chamber / Community:** easiest live practice scene. The hard part is ensuring sourced context, revised words, and the export matter more than the timer. No official hearing or submission is simulated as real.

These comparative judgments are design recommendations, not high-confidence empirical findings.

## Contradictions and resolutions

1. **Original Reclaim recommendation vs Block Ops direction:** the user added participation and aura preferences, changing the recommendation, then explicitly selected Block Ops. The underlying research was retained; the choice is now a recorded user decision.
2. **October 24 deadline vs Friday submission target:** 12:00 a.m. on October 24 is the start of Saturday. Submit Friday evening with a buffer. Recheck the live event clock before submission.
3. **Judging 3–5 p.m. vs a 3 p.m. winners label:** unresolved schedule wording. Preserve both as a logistics question rather than guessing.
4. **Multiple closed reports vs an unresolved neighborhood problem:** not equivalent. The snapshot supports a record history, not a physical-condition conclusion.
5. **A deliberately wrong summary vs measured AI bias:** not equivalent. The preview mutation is disclosed and cannot support a bias claim.
6. **A useful-looking packet vs a useful real-world workflow:** unresolved. No applicant, clinician, coordinator, or resident group has validated the proposed outputs.

## Methodology

The prior work compared 17 concepts across all three tracks using parallel track research and a critical synthesis. It reviewed the event rubric and schedule, official public APIs and policy pages, primary research, and relevant competitor documentation. A fresh bounded DC service query was saved on October 20; interactive previews were checked on October 21.

This synthesis uses those local artifacts as its evidence base. It does not represent a new full web crawl. Ruflo memory search, neural prediction, and remote trajectory tools were unavailable in the current tool inventory; no remote memory result or computed relevance score is claimed. [Research index](../research/README.md), [source index](../research/SOURCES.md), and [import manifest](../research/evidence/import-manifest.json) preserve the trail.

## Limitations

No community interviews, practitioner review, real applicant/patient records, production security review, measured model experiment, or outcome study has been completed. The available sources establish problem mechanisms and prior art more strongly than unmet demand for our particular designs. Competition duplication cannot be ruled out. Schedule, API availability, provider behavior, and policy details can change after the recorded research dates.

## Recommendations

1. Implement the recorded Block Ops selection; do not reopen the finalist choice. Luna completes its technical plan in Phase 1.
2. Prove the selected dependency in a short day-one experiment: real public data for Block Ops, reviewed paired runs for The Switch, source-linked extraction for Casebreaker, or a complete controlled fixture flow for the others.
3. Complete input → human decision → export on day one. Cut features before sacrificing that loop.
4. Ask one relevant reviewer to assess the output if available. This is a proposed next step, not completed outreach.
5. Measure source accuracy, successful corrections, abstention, and export fidelity. State downstream outcomes only if actually evaluated.
